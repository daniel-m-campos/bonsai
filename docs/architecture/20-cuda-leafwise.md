## The claim

Device leafwise does not need new histogram, partition, or split-scan math. It needs a different histogram storage discipline (a per-tree slot pool instead of the per-level ping-pong), a persistent per-leaf segment map instead of the per-level layout vectors, and slot indirection in the one kernel that lacks it. The existing kernels are already frontier-width-agnostic where it matters: `hist_kernel` and `hist_small_kernel` take a slot table, the partition chain is segment-local (one op touches only its own `[offset, offset+count)` range), and `find_kernel` needs the same slot table `hist_kernel` already has. The new engine surface is a second concept beside `GPULevelEngine`, not a change to it; the depthwise and oblivious paths stay exactly as decisions 53/71/72 left them.

The performance thesis comes from reading LightGBM's CUDA learner (`src/treelearner/cuda/` at master, 2026-08): its 24.4 s at 16M x 100 x 100 iters x 256 leaves is not a bandwidth result but a per-split-round constant of roughly 950 us times 25,500 rounds (255 splits per tree x 100 trees). The constant is fixed overhead that does not shrink as leaves shrink: a hard-coded minimum histogram grid of 160 row-blocks per feature partition (`cuda_histogram_constructor.hpp`, `min_grid_dim_y_`), so every round pays ~8M global double atomics in the shared-to-global epilogue regardless of leaf size; 8 full `cudaDeviceSynchronize` per round; ~16 kernel launches per round, four of which run 6 to 20 threads; and a full-width histogram subtraction and split scan every round. bonsai's plane has none of these by construction: grids track node size, the per-level sync floor is one pinned D2H (doc 16), and the find scans only selected features. A leafwise plane that keeps per-round fixed cost near 100 us lands the same histogram volume as `cuda_depthwise` (the subtraction trick makes total work identical at matched knobs) plus ~2.5 s of serialization overhead at 16M, comfortably under 24.4 s against the 17.2 s depthwise anchor on the issue #268 pod.

The adopt/refuse reading of LightGBM's CUDA learner moved to [the performance engineering chapter](../guide/11-performance-engineering.md).

## The plane

A new `LeafPipeline` beside `LevelPipeline` in the device context, sharing `DeviceData` (the binned matrix, per-dataset) and `GradientPlane` (the interleaved `float2`, per-tree) unchanged.

**Histogram pool.** One buffer of `num_leaves * n_selected * stride` doubles (`stride = 2 * max_sel_bins`, same layout as today's slots), memset once per tree. At the issue #268 knobs (256 leaves, 100 features, 255 bins) that is 104 MB, the same as LightGBM's pool. Slot assignment: root takes slot 0; each split builds the smaller child into the next free slot (monotonic counter) and subtracts in place in the parent's slot, which becomes the larger child's slot. A new `subtract_inplace_kernel` variant reads and writes the same buffer (today's `subtract_kernel` assumes parent and child in different buffers); the hist kernels need nothing, they already write through `out_slot[]` indirection onto zeroed memory.

**Segment map.** `slot_offsets` and `slot_counts` become persistent per-leaf host vectors keyed by slot id, updated one entry at a time, instead of per-level vectors rebuilt wholesale by `layout_children`. The row array keeps its invariant: a permutation of `[0, n)` where every live leaf owns a contiguous range. One split rewrites exactly one range into two adjacent subranges.

As built, the map lives in a `LeafPipeline` beside the level plane's, and the slot counter advances only when both children survive the partition: a split the host demotes on an empty child spends no slot, so slot demand is exactly the live leaf count and the pool bound stays `num_leaves`. Rows, gradients, flags, the leaf assignment array, and the per-tree knobs (selected features, stride) are the level pipeline's buffers reused as they are; the pool and this map are all the leaf plane adds.

**Non-swapping partition.** The existing `route_count_kernel` / `seg_scan_kernel` / `scatter_kernel` chain runs with `n_ops = 1`, scattering the parent's range into the same offsets of the other row buffer, followed by a D2D copy of that range back into the primary buffer (rows plus the ordered `float2` gradients that travel with them). The b-side row and gradient buffers the level plane uses for ping-pong become this scratch; no new allocation. Cost: two extra passes over the split node's rows, zero sibling traffic, no buffer flip. `leaf_by_row` stamping and the tree epilogue (`finalize_rows`, `finalize_tree`) are reusable as is once the segment map is persistent.

**Find with slot indirection.** `find_kernel` currently addresses histograms as `node * n_sel + sel`, assuming the frontier occupies slots 0..n-1 contiguously. It gains the same `slot[]` table argument `hist_kernel` has, so a launch over the two new children names their actual pool slots. The scan math, tie-break, and `reduce_kernel` are untouched, preserving the single definition of splitting semantics shared with the CPU finders.

**Root.** The GPU `LevelStep::make_root` returns a root whose host-side histogram and row views are empty, so the CPU splitter would produce single-leaf trees; the leaf plane cannot reuse it. `leaf_begin_root` uploads the root segment, builds slot 0, and runs the device find on it, returning the root's `FeatBest` to seed the heap.

As built, `leaf_begin_root` shares the level plane's identity-permutation cache through an extracted `stage_root_rows` and reduces the root totals with the same deterministic two-pass kernels, over the gathered segment in every case rather than the level plane's three-way branch on identity, resident mode, and explicit row list. The find is a separate `leaf_find` call over slot 0, the same entry point the two children use per round.

## The round

Per heap pop, in launch order on one stream:

1. Host stages one `PartOpDev` for the popped leaf (20 B H2D).
2. Partition chain (3 kernels) plus the range copy-back (1 D2D); child counts come down (8 B, sync 1). The host now knows smaller and larger.
3. Host stages the hist triple; `hist_kernel` or `hist_small_kernel` (by the existing 512-row policy) builds the smaller child into a fresh slot; `subtract_inplace_kernel` derives the larger in the parent's slot.
4. Host stages the two children's sums and bounds (32 B up, from the split output it already holds); `find_kernel` + `reduce_kernel` over the two children; two `FeatBest` come down (112 B, sync 2).
5. Host pushes both children into the gain heap and continues. Heap order, tie-break, monotone and interaction propagation, empty-child demotion, the leaf budget, and the depth cap all stay in the grower, unchanged from CPU leafwise.

That is 6 to 7 launches, 2 pinned syncs, and under 200 B of bus traffic per round. The budget that decides admission is the fixed per-round cost F: fit time is roughly the depthwise compute volume plus `rounds x F`, with `rounds = (leaves - 1) x iters` = 25,500 at the protocol knobs. At F = 100 us that is 2.6 s of overhead at any scale; against the 17.2 s depthwise anchor it prices a ~20 s fit at 16M, beating 24.4 s. At 250k rows, where lgbm_cuda fits in 5.7 s, the same 2.6 s of overhead sits on top of ~0.5 s of compute: still ahead, but it shows why F is the number to defend. LightGBM's F is ~950 us; the design goal is an order of magnitude below that, and stage 0 measures it before any plane code is written.

The known risk is occupancy, not sync latency. Deep in a 256-leaf tree the median split touches tens of thousands of rows; at 32k-row chunks a 31k-row node launches `n_selected` blocks total, underfilling an L40S's 142 SMs. Two mitigations, in order: shrink the chunk size as node size falls so the grid stays wide (more cross-chunk merge traffic, bounded and proportional to actual rows, never a fixed floor), and the small-node kernel already covers the tail. The first was built and measured on the unmerged `perf/leafwise-occupancy` branch after stage 3 closed, and it does not pay at 100 selected features; the lever list below carries the numbers and the regime where it does. If admission shows the plane compute-bound on mid-size nodes anyway, the priced fallback is batched expansion: pop the top K heap entries and run one K-node launch through the same multi-node kernels. That is an algorithm change, not a tuning knob, because strict best-first order can differ when a child of the first pop would have outranked the second pop; it would ship, if ever, as a measured separate mode, never as a silent default, since the parity contract with CPU leafwise (tolerance-equal predictions, decision 40's contract extended) only absorbs rounding differences, not ordering differences. Speculative pipelining of round i+1's histogram under round i's partition is compatible with this plane (LightGBM's barriers foreclose it; ours don't) and is deferred alongside CUDA graphs to a post-admission optimization round.

## The seam

A new `GPULeafEngine` concept beside `GPULevelEngine` in `grower.hpp`. The sketch's proposed names, none of which reached the code, were `leaf_begin_tree`, `leaf_begin_root` (returning the accept/decline bool and the root's split), `split_leaf` (partition + counts), `build_leaf` (hist + subtract + find on the two children), plus a shared `stamp_leaves` / `finalize_rows` / `finalize_tree` epilogue; the paragraph after this one gives the five calls that shipped, and those are the ones to grep for. `LeafwiseGrower<CudaHistogramEngine>` follows the level plane's one-runtime-fork pattern: `leaf_begin_root` declines wholesale (same `hist_budget_ok` predicate, plus a pool-budget check on `num_leaves * slot_doubles`) and the existing CPU `split_node` path takes over; there is no per-node CPU fallback on the resident path. Decision 41's finding stands: no `LevelPlan` unification, no new typelist dimension; the grower composes engine calls directly, exactly as the CPU leafwise composes `level_step.hpp` primitives today.

As built, the engine surface is five calls: `leaf_begin_root`, `leaf_split` (partition, counts, and slot assignment), `leaf_build` (hist plus in-place subtract), `leaf_find` (any set of named slots, root or children), and `leaf_stamp`; `begin_tree` and `finalize_tree` are the level plane's, unchanged. Two refinements over the sketch. First, the grow loop reaches all of it through a `LeafStep` shaped exactly like `LevelStep`, host primary template and GPU specialization, rather than calling the engine directly: leafwise had no seam at all before this, since `LevelStep` only ever gave it `make_root`. Second, `leaf_split` and `leaf_build` are separate calls because the host's empty-child demotion decides between them, the same order the depthwise plan uses (`apply_level`, then `demote_empty_splits`, then `build_children`). The pool-budget check is against a quarter of the device's free memory, read from `cudaMemGetInfo`. At the depth cap the children's build and find are both skipped: their histograms would never be read.

A `SplitInput` node carries its row count one of two ways depending on which plane produced it: host-plane nodes carry the rows themselves in `rows`, device-plane nodes carry only the count in `row_count` and leave `rows` empty; `is_empty_child` and `row_count_of` (`src/level_step.hpp`) are the sanctioned readers of that dual contract, and every other call site should go through them rather than repeating the check.

Out of scope for stage 1, wired later: the device-resident objective (the resident plane's per-fit machinery is orthogonal and joined after admission, as stage 3's first lever below), and multi-GPU (doc 19 is parked; nothing here forecloses it).

As built, the resident seam is `LeafwiseGrower::resident_begin` forwarding to `resident_begin_leaf`, and `LeafStep::end_tree` routing through the same `resident_finalize` the level plane uses: the finished tree flattens once for both planes, the device routes every row in bin space and fuses the score update, and nothing is stamped because nothing reads a leaf assignment. The grow loop then allocates no per-row `values`/`leaf_ids` and skips the out-of-bag routing, which the device route+add covers for sampled and unsampled rows alike.

## Memory and decline gates

The pool is the only new allocation: `num_leaves * n_selected * stride * 8` bytes (104 MB at the protocol knobs; 2.1 GB at 2048 columns x 256 leaves x 255 bins). The decline predicate extends `hist_budget_ok` with the pool bound so oversized configurations fall back to CPU leafwise wholesale at `leaf_begin_root`, visible to both callers, per the existing single-predicate rule. Both gates live in one function, `leaf_budget_ok`, because the resident objective needs to evaluate them once per fit rather than per tree: a tree that declined to the host plane after the gradients stopped crossing the bus would have no host gradients to read. `resident_begin_leaf` therefore applies the conservative bound, every feature selected and the widest feature sizing the stride, which is the largest pool any tree can ask for. A tree's selection is always a subset, so clearing the conservative bound leaves no tree able to decline, and `feature_fraction` can only make the conservative bound larger than a given tree's, refusing arming rather than permitting a mid-fit decline; at the protocol cell (100 features, no subsampling) the two are the same test. The pool bound additionally cannot be retested per tree once armed, since the pool the first tree allocates shrinks the free memory a later tree would measure, so `leaf_begin_root` skips the gate while the resident mode is armed. Rows, gradients, flags, and the leaf assignment array are the level plane's buffers reused; peak device memory does not exceed the depthwise path's at equal knobs plus the pool delta (the level plane's transient 2n-slot histogram high-water mark at the widest level is comparable to the pool at matched leaf budgets).

## Profiling and measurement discipline

Per-round laps must replicate the sync peel decisions 62 and 72 were bitten by: a lap that opens without draining the previous round's kernels absorbs them. The `BONSAI_CUDA_PROFILE` accumulator pattern (event pairs recorded at launch, read at the next profile sync) extends with per-round buckets: partition, hist, subtract, find, and the host residue, so F is directly observable, not inferred.

**As measured at stage 2, corrected by the stage 3 diagnosis** (16M x 100, one L40S; the profiled rep behind decision 97, re-instrumented with per-kernel cudaEvents on `perf/leafwise-diag`). Grow is 19.7-20.3 s against resident depthwise's 10.8 s, and the stage 2 attribution was wrong in two ways. First, `find_stage`'s 9.80 s was 84% device compute in disguise: `leaf_find`'s first pageable staging copy stream-syncs, so it absorbed the in-flight histogram and subtract kernels (8.2 s) that the sibling planes peel into `gpu_wait`. The histogram itself runs at depthwise throughput (8.99 s vs 8.77 s device time for the same volume), so there is no leafwise histogram penalty at this leaf budget and level batching buys nothing here. Second, the comparison itself was uneven: depthwise ran with the device-resident objective and leafwise, at that point, could not arm it, and a same-pod kill-switch A/B prices that seam at 5.90 s (per-tree 128 MB gradient uploads in `setup`, 128 MB values and leaf_ids downloads in the epilogue, and the host allocations around them). The true leaf-plane penalty is 3.6 s: ~200 us per round of device idle during true host staging (ceiling 2.5 s), the partition chain's copy-back and single-threaded scan (0.5-0.9 s), and residue.

The corrected lever list, now closed (decision 98). Landed: the resident objective wired into leafwise growth (5.6 s of fit measured, lever 1 below); pinned and asynchronous round staging, including dropping the fit-constant monotone re-upload that repeated on every find (a fixed 0.37 s of grow at every scale, lever 2 below). Refuted: the partition chain, built and measured and reverted for no win outside the noise (lever 3 below). Never opened, and why: CUDA graphs are the heavy form of lever 2 and are not required for parity; adaptive accumulator width is a global histogram question rather than a leafwise gap-closer, since the planes' histogram times already match; level batching buys nothing at this leaf budget for the same reason. Measured on the unmerged `perf/leafwise-occupancy` branch after the close and refused: small-node occupancy, priced in the lever 4 paragraphs below, which also correct the condition this list had wrong. Still gated: the speculative pipelining named above, round i+1's histogram under round i's partition, needs the round's staging fences made explicit first (the `PinnedStaged` aliasing contract), so that reordering the round cannot race the pinned buffers. The two landed levers priced leafwise at 1.3 s of fit from resident depthwise, and the closing ladder below is where that number is read.

**Lever 1 as built** (2026-08-02, one L40S, 16M x 100 at the protocol knobs, the two arms interleaved on the same pod, two reps each, `BONSAI_CUDA_PROFILE=1 BONSAI_GROW_PROFILE=1 BONSAI_FIT_PROFILE=1`). Median fit falls from 30.23 s to 24.60 s and median grow from 20.02 s to 13.13 s, against the same pod's resident depthwise anchor at 22.65 s fit and 10.86 s grow; `r2_test` is identical at 0.8793 on every run. The grow delta reads larger than the fit delta because 1.90 s of it is the one-time binned-matrix upload relocating out of the grow lap into `resident_begin`, exactly where it sits on the depthwise plane; the per-tree savings are the gradient uploads (`setup` 3.3 s to 0.0 s, of which 1.3 s is the gh upload), the values and leaf-id downloads (`fin_d2h` 1.3 s to 0.0 s), and the host objective and score loops (0.34 s). What separates leafwise from depthwise afterwards is 2.3 s of grow, which is the two remaining levers.

**Lever 2 as built, and lever 3 as priced and withdrawn** (2026-08-02, one L40S, the same protocol cell and knobs, arms interleaved on the same pod from three builds of the three commits, and with no profiler: the profiled configuration is the wrong instrument here, for the reason below). Lever 2 is the round's staging: the fit-constant monotone vector uploads once per tree instead of once per find, the histogram kernels' (offset, count, slot) triple becomes one packed upload behind three pointers into it, and what remains moves to pinned host memory and asynchronous copies, four per round against the previous eight pageable ones. It saves a fixed 0.37 s of grow per fit, which is 14.5 us of the round against 25,500 rounds, and the saving is the same absolute number at every scale because it is per round, not per row. Ten interleaved blocks at 16M x 100: fit median 20.69 s to 20.39 s and grow 14.45 s to 14.12 s, a paired median of -0.377 s of fit (-1.8%) and -0.372 s of grow (-2.6%), grow lower in 10 blocks out of 10. Three blocks at 1M: fit 3.14 s to 2.79 s (-11.0%), grow 2.68 s to 2.31 s (-13.7%). Three blocks at 250k: fit 2.27 s to 1.88 s (-17.1%), grow 2.13 s to 1.73 s (-18.9%). `r2_test` is identical to the baseline at all three cells (0.8793, 0.8771, 0.8718). The same-pod `cuda_depthwise` anchor is unmoved (grow 12.58 s and 12.60 s before, 12.60 s and 12.58 s after), as it must be: `Staged` keeps its pageable semantics and the level plane's staging is untouched.

Lever 3 was built and reverted. A parallel tiled scan replaced the single-threaded segment scan, and the range copy-back moved to a non-blocking stream under the round's histogram, which read the smaller child from the scratch side the scatter had just written. Against its own parent it moves the fit median +0.03% at 16M, -1.2% at 1M, and +0.3% at 250k, with grow -0.8% at 16M; its gate was 2% of fit, so both halves were withdrawn rather than carried as a stream and an event nobody paid for. The lesson generalizes: the 0.5 to 0.9 s the diagnosis attributed to the copy-back and the scan is real device time, but it overlaps work already in flight, so removing it buys back only the fraction that was on the critical path.

That correction applies to the diagnosis itself. The ~200 us per round of device idle it measured, and the 2.5 s ceiling read off it, came from a profiled run, and `leaf_find`'s profile peel drains the pipeline once per round by construction. The drain is what makes the round's host residue visible, and it is also what makes it expensive: unprofiled, the same staging costs 14.5 us per round, not 100. Profiling stays the right instrument for attributing device time between kernels; it is the wrong one for pricing host residue on a plane whose rounds are this short, and any future leaf-plane lever should be gated on unprofiled wall clock at more than one scale.

**The closing ladder** (2026-08-02, one L40S, `leafwise-stage3-2026-08.jsonl` in [the archive](../method/results/archive.md), decision 98, unprofiled, three device arms, best of two reps, interleaved). At the protocol knobs `cuda_leafwise` fits 250k in 2.1 s, 1M in 3.2 s, 4M in 7.6 s, and 16M in 24.4 s, against lgbm_cuda's 6.6/7.6/12.3/31.9 s and the resident depthwise anchor's 0.7/1.6/5.9/23.0 s. That is a 21% to 27% cut on stage 2's leafwise column, largest at the small cells where the round's fixed cost is the fit; the reference arms reproduce their stage 2 times within 2.4% at every cell, which is what licenses reading the cut as the levers, since the two ladders are different rentals of the same GPU model. Uncapped at 16M the arm reads 32.3 s at r2 0.8862 against LightGBM's 31.7 s at 0.8858, down from 38.6 s: the lead at matched accuracy shrinks from 22% to 2%, and the residue is the structural one, that uncapped best-first finds a split for every leaf it creates and so pays 51,100 rounds against the capped tree's 25,500. Both readings in this paragraph are handicapped by the ingest path decision 100 corrects; re-measured there, the capped cell reads 18.7 s against 37.0 s and the uncapped cell 27.5 s against 36.8 s, which reverses the matched-accuracy lead. The round-count fact is unchanged, and it is the only part of this paragraph the correction leaves standing.

**Lever 4 as built and refused, on branch `perf/leafwise-occupancy`, unmerged** (2026-08-02, one L40S, unprofiled, two builds of two commits interleaved on the same pod, all arms at identical `r2_test`). The lever sizes the leaf plane's histogram row-chunks from the device instead of fixing them at 32,768 rows: it shrinks the chunk toward a 4096-row floor until a node's grid reaches two blocks per streaming multiprocessor, and every chunk it adds still carries rows, so no node pays a grid it does not use. The level plane keeps the wide chunk. On the pre-registered cell, 16M x 100 at 1024 leaves, uncapped depth, and 30 iterations, four interleaved blocks read a median fit of 18.86 s before and 18.94 s after, and a median grow of 13.43 s and 13.55 s: a 0.5% loss of fit against a gate of 5% gained. The protocol cell is flat (19.26 s to 19.25 s of fit, inside its 1% no-regression bound) and the `cuda_depthwise` anchor is unmoved (17.78 s to 17.72 s), which is the check that the level plane was untouched. An arm four times more aggressive (eight blocks per SM, a 1024-row floor) reads 19.49 s, a 3.3% loss of fit on the same cell, so the direction is monotone: at 100 selected features the cross-chunk global merge costs more than the extra blocks return.

The same run corrects the condition. The grid is selected features times chunks, so the occupancy deficit is set by the feature width and not by node size alone, and 100 features already put 100 blocks on the device before a single extra chunk. At 20 selected features and the same deep knobs the identical lever cuts fit 11.1% (7.59 s to 6.75 s) and grow 13.1% (6.12 s to 5.32 s) at identical `r2_test`, and the aggressive arm buys nothing beyond it (6.78 s of fit), so two blocks per SM is the right target wherever the lever pays at all. A shipping form would have to gate on `n_selected` rather than on a node's rows, and no cell on the scale protocol is narrow enough to want one. The item closes as refused at the widths bonsai measures, with the narrow-column price recorded for whoever meets that regime; the implementation itself stays on `perf/leafwise-occupancy`, unmerged, for whoever has that workload.

## Admission

Feature-admission applies; the burden of proof is on the plane.

- **Stage 0, price F before building.** A harness that replays the round cadence (7 launches on representative grids, 2 pinned syncs, host staging) 25,500 times on the target L40S. If F lands above ~300 us, the thesis is wrong and the design returns to the batched-K question before any engine code exists.
- **Stage 1, parity. Done.** The plane behind `cuda_leafwise`, tolerance-equal against CPU `leafwise` (prediction/RMSE tolerance, never tree equality), depth cap honored, decline paths exercised, `bonsai info` truthful about where histograms are computed. The decision 42 anti-goal is the test: no CPU histogram work under the `cuda_` name. Profiling buckets reuse the level plane's counters; the per-round `F` decomposition the section above asks for arrives with stage 2, where it is the number under measurement.
- **Stage 2, the ladder. Done, passed (decision 97).** Same-pod L40S: `cuda_leafwise` vs `lgbm_cuda` vs CPU `leafwise` vs the `cuda_depthwise` anchor at 250k/1M/4M/16M x 100, protocol knobs, plus one uncapped-depth arm (explicit `num_leaves`, no depth cap) where leaf-wise structurally differs and where lgbm_cuda's ignored depth cap makes the comparison honest. The kill criterion is issue #268's: beat lgbm_cuda at 16M x 100 on the same pod, or do not register. 30.8 s against 32.4 s; met. `cuda_leafwise` must also beat same-pod CPU leafwise everywhere it registers; a device grower that loses to its own CPU arm does not ship. Met at every cell, 4.6x to 7.1x. The measured ladder and its uncapped arm are on the scale results page. Decision 100 re-measures the criterion on the fixed ingest path: it is met by 2.0x to 2.3x rather than the margin recorded here.

- **Stage 3, the levers. Done, closed (decision 98).** Not an admission gate: the plane was already registered, so this stage is the optimization round the admission left open, run under the perf-round discipline. Two levers landed against a pre-registered 2% of fit, one was reverted for missing it, and the stage 2 diagnosis was corrected. The closing ladder is the three device arms at the same knobs plus the uncapped 16M arm, on the scale results page. 24.4 s against lgbm_cuda's 31.9 s at 16M, 1.3 s from the depthwise anchor. On the fixed ingest path (decision 100) the same cell reads 18.7 s against 37.0 s, and the distance to the depthwise anchor is 2.9 s: the handicap sat on both planes and flattered it.
